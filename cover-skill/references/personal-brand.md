# Personal Creator Signature

These are hard identity rules for the owner's recurring creator covers. They always apply inside `cover-skill` and preserve a recognizable personal system without fixing one palette, font, texture, or layout. A request to remove them must use a different workflow rather than weakening this Skill.

## Required identity ingredients

- Use the real portrait and real kangaroo IP as identity sources for every Stage 1 concept and selected-ratio output. The final visible subject must be an approved integrated portrait-pet master, not the raw IP bitmap.
- If either asset is unavailable after inspecting the current directory and supplied attachments, stop and request it. Do not silently replace it with a generic person or mascot.
- Treat the product or APP Logo as a third required identity ingredient when the user supplies one. Never ask an image model to redraw it.

## Portrait expression

- Choose and record one expression intent from the current title and topic: `surprised`, `questioning`, or `surprised-questioning`. Use `user-specified-neutral` only when the user explicitly requests a neutral expression.
- A neutral presenter expression is a hard failure unless the user explicitly requests neutral.
- Preserve the user's identity, glasses, hairstyle, skin tone, and recognizable facial proportions. Expression editing may change eyebrows, eyes, mouth, head angle, and gaze only as needed for the chosen intent.
- Before composing A–D, create one portrait-expression master from the real portrait and inspect it visually for both identity and the chosen emotion. A neutral source portrait cannot be reused directly. Reuse the approved master across all four concepts so identity and emotion stay consistent.
- Protect the entire central face area from text, Logos, mascot overlap, decorative graphics, and platform crops.

## Kangaroo relationship

- The kangaroo is a beloved pet physically resting on the creator, not a Logo pasted near the creator. Before layout, generate and visually approve an integrated portrait-pet master with pose `shoulder` or `head`; use that combined subject in the cover.
- A shoulder pet shows the recognizable head/ears and enough upper body for the pose, with paws or chest visibly bearing weight on the shoulder or clothing. Its lower torso is naturally hidden behind the shoulder/back; tight contact shadow, useful occlusion, subtle cloth compression/folds, matched perspective, and matched light make the contact believable.
- A head pet lies or crawls across the top of the hair, with paws or body visibly bearing weight. Part of the pet is hidden behind hair or the crown; bent/compressed hair, tight contact shadow, matched perspective, and matched light make the contact believable.
- The raw full-body IP bitmap is `reference-only`. Cropping, keying, resizing, or pasting it on the shoulder/head is a hard failure, even if the coordinates are correct. Also fail a complete standing/sitting body, visible ground feet or tail, floating gap, independent platform, rectangular edge, sticker outline, or pose with no weight/contact evidence.
- It may additionally interact with the title or product, but that interaction never replaces the required shoulder-or-head physical support.
- Never let the kangaroo or another element cover the user's face. Never let a Logo cover the kangaroo's eyes, nose, mouth, or central face.

## Center-weighted subject and typography

- Make the portrait and current product subject the main visual anchor. Default to centered or center-weighted composition rather than a generic text-left/person-right split.
- Across all four concepts and every adapted ratio, the portrait/product anchor must remain centered or center-weighted. Asymmetry may exist inside the composition, but it must not turn into a text-left/person-right split.
- Title and subject must share depth. Place suitable title portions behind hair, shoulders, hands, the kangaroo, or a product object, and other portions in front, while keeping every glyph unmistakable.
- Do not solve the layout with one large text panel plus unrelated stickers. Floating UI cards, empty modules, and badge fragments require evidence from the current product reference; otherwise they are template noise.
- The title must read as one coherent headline. Do not demote an opening clause such as `刚刚，` into an unrelated micro-badge merely to fill space.

## Copy hierarchy

- The title is the dominant reading target. The first title sentence or primary clause receives the largest type; later qualification may be smaller but still belongs to the headline.
- The summary is clearly secondary, not footnote-sized. At the resolved target review thumbnail, its CJK glyph height should normally be about 11–13 px or greater and the entire sentence must remain readable with deliberate attention; use the upper end when space permits.
- Choose type color, weight, width, outline, texture, and shadow from the current topic and uploaded product visual language. Yellow is not a default.

## Product Logo integration

- Make the real product Logo recognizable at thumbnail size and large enough to matter.
- Integrate it into the subject, title lockup, product object, or a deliberate branded module. Do not default to a tiny top-corner badge.
- Preserve its aspect ratio, core shape, internal colors, and text or symbol. Decorative rings, glows, or containers may support it only when they do not alter or crowd the mark.

## Style freedom

- Re-decide palette, font personality, texture, lighting, and illustration treatment for every topic from the title, product Logo, APP screenshot, and emotional tone.
- The signature is the relationship among portrait, expression, kangaroo, title, and Logo—not a permanent red-black palette, paper texture, fixed font, or repeated card layout.
