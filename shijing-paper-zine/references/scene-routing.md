# Scene routing

## Everyday objects, food, and still life

- Preserve the object arrangement, touch, use, or small imperfection that makes the snapshot personal.
- Use table, wall, cloth, floor, packaging-free surface, or shadow as the quiet field.
- Simplify repeated utensils, dishes, flowers, books, cables, or clutter into one or two broad rhythms.
- Let structural color grow from a real object, reflection, ingredient, fabric, or light source.
- Keep the result observational and intimate, not like an e-commerce advertisement.

Typical failure: turning a casual object into a polished product ad. Correct by restoring the original placement, ordinary surface, and one lived-in detail.

## Portraits, pets, and personal moments

- Preserve identity, gesture, gaze, distance, and the relationship between subject and surroundings.
- Default to pixel-preserving mode for recognizable faces, pets, clothing graphics, or personal objects.
- Use negative space around the subject instead of filling the page with decorative marks.
- Extend one gesture, gaze direction, shadow, leash, sleeve, chair, or environmental line into the paper field.
- Keep skin, fur, and expression natural; do not beautify or editorialize the person into a fashion model.

Typical failure: the person becomes posed, retouched, or emotionally different. Correct by keeping the source pixels unchanged and moving the design treatment to the surrounding paper field.

## Interiors and domestic spaces

- Preserve one spatial relationship: window to table, chair to light, doorway to room, shelf to wall, or person to furniture.
- Remove scattered small objects that do not support the eye path.
- Use wall, curtain, floor, ceiling, or daylight as the quiet field.
- Let structural color follow light, a textile, furniture edge, or architectural plane.
- Avoid turning an ordinary room into an aspirational showroom.

Typical failure: a generic lifestyle interior replaces the real space. Correct by restoring the original room geometry and one recognizable object.

## Landscape and waterfront

- Favor horizon, shoreline, path, ridge, reflection, or one distant figure.
- Convert sky, water, haze, or empty foreground into the main quiet field.
- Merge crowds, waves, trees, and buildings into a few broad rhythms.
- Let structural color continue water, sky, clothing, light, or a path.
- Good micro-copy names movement, distance, weather, or silence.

Typical failure: turning the whole landscape into a detailed print. Correct by preserving one photographic anchor and removing most environmental marks.

## Architecture and city streets

- Preserve vanishing point, street canyon, façade rhythm, landmark position, or old/new contrast.
- Remove unreadable signs, parked vehicles, cables, and repeated windows when they compete with direction.
- Use sparse contour or dry-print blocks; never redraw every window.
- Let structural color follow a road, traffic signal, roofline, shadow, or repeated architectural plane.
- Leave breathing room on the side opposite the visual weight.

Typical failure: a stylish façade with no connection to the source. Correct by restoring the source vanishing point and one unmistakable landmark relationship.

## Humanistic and daily-life scenes

- Preserve the action and its relationship to place: talking under a tree, working beside a stall, waiting at a station, walking through rain.
- Use pixel-preserving mode when recognizable faces matter.
- Prefer environmental portraits, back views, distant figures, or gestures when using generative reconstruction.
- Simplify crowds into spacing and direction rather than new detailed people.
- Derive supporting drawing from tools, furniture, foliage, cages, umbrellas, awnings, or paths already present.
- Keep the tone observational, not sentimental or picturesque.

Typical failure: regenerated people no longer match the source. Correct by compositing the original photo unchanged or choosing a source without identity-critical faces.

## Source selection

Prefer photographs with:

- one clear anchor;
- one dominant direction;
- a natural quiet region;
- visible contrast between dense and calm areas;
- a meaningful minor color that can be intensified.

Use extra caution with tightly cropped group portraits, document screenshots, product labels that must stay exact, photographs filled edge-to-edge with equal detail, and images whose value depends on readable signage. When such a source must be used, keep it unchanged in pixel-preserving mode and apply the style around it rather than regenerating it.
